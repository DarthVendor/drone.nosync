# Drone frames — CAD, print guides, mass properties

Mechanical design only. No controller or simulator code — the mass and inertia
numbers are here so you can plug them into your own sim.

```
drone_frames/
├── delivery/      650 mm, 1.5 kg payload  [primary]
├── micro_frame/   88 g coreless           [bench article]
└── import/        mesh + inertia + URDF/MJCF for your sim
```

Every part exports as both **STEP** (parametric — open in Fusion, FreeCAD,
Onshape, SolidWorks to modify) and **STL** (slice and print). All models are
generated by the CadQuery scripts alongside them, so changing wheelbase, prop
size, or motor pattern is a constant at the top of a file plus a re-run.

```bash
pip install cadquery trimesh
python delivery_frame.py     # hub, arm clamps, motor mounts
python payload_bay.py        # cardan gimbal + bay bracket
python release_mech.py       # winch drum, release hook, gate, fairlead
python delivery_render.py    # shaded views + inertia-vs-swing plot
```

## `delivery/` — 650 mm, 15" props, 1.5 kg takeout

3.84 kg empty, 5.34 kg AUW, T/W 1.95 loaded. 79 mm prop tip clearance.

**Arms are carbon tube, not printed.** Each carries 2.6 kg of thrust at a
325 mm moment arm, cycling at rotor frequency for the whole flight. A printed
arm at that scale is a fatigue crack running along layer lines — it survives the
bench test and fails in the air. Printed parts are joints only.

| part | qty | PETG | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `hub_plate` | 2 | 176 g | sandwich hub, arm clamp seats |
| `arm_clamp` | 8 | 29 g | two halves per 25 mm tube |
| `motor_mount` | 4 | 21 g | tube end, pinch bolt, M3 25 mm pattern |
| `cardan_outer` | 1 | 73 g | U-yoke under hub, roll axis |
| `cardan_cross` | 1 | 83 g | cross link, roll + pitch pins |
| `bay_bracket` | 1 | 143 g | pitch pivots up, flange to box |
| `winch_drum` | 1 | 81 g | D-flat keys to gearmotor |
| `release_hook_body` | 1 | 22 g | throat holds the payload bail |
| `release_gate` | 1 | 3 g | counterweighted gate |
| `tether_guide` | 1 | 15 g | fairlead under hub |
| `gimbal_ring` | — | 52 g | **superseded** by the cardan, see below |

Print settings, all parts: PETG or PA-CF, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers,
**6 perimeters**, 40% gyroid, 245/85 °C, 30 mm/s, brim, no supports. Perimeter
count carries the load, not infill. PA-CF is worth a hardened nozzle here in a
way it is not at 88 g — these are load-bearing joints.

### Two things the analysis changed

**The payload goes in a rigid box, not a bag.** A hanging plastic bag sits in a
13.7 m/s downwash wake; inflated it sees 28.9 N of unsteady load — twice the
payload's own weight — roughly doubles cruise drag, and can billow up into the
disc. `gimbal_ring` was also undersized: a real order (three clamshells plus two
drinks) needs 280 × 240 × 260 mm, which would have meant a 400 mm ring on
outriggers. The compact cardan does the same job in a 114 mm footprint.

**Landing gear needs to be 410 mm.** Box height plus cardan stack plus ground
clearance. Build it as a wide skid frame rather than four legs, and re-check the
empty CoM. Free swing before a box corner reaches the gear is 28.3° — put hard
stops in the cardan at ±25°.

### Release

Tether tension holds the gate shut against a 2.8 N·mm counterweight moment. On
touchdown the tether goes slack, tension drops below ~0.15 N, the gate rotates
open and the bail slides out. **Fails safe by geometry** — the gate cannot open
under load, so release requires weight-off, which cannot happen at altitude. No
actuator at the hook, no wire down the tether.

Top hazard is a snag on retract: a caught hook tethers the aircraft to the
ground and it will pull itself down. Fit a tension limiter and an emergency
cutter. For first flights, skip the winch and use land-and-release with a servo
latch on the bay.

## `micro_frame/` — 88 g coreless

Single-piece printed frame, 110 mm wheelbase, 65 mm props, press-fit 8520
motors, sized around Feather STM32F405 + DRV8833 + VL53L1X. Guards and legs in
TPU because they are the impact parts.

Worth printing as a bench article for fit checks and system-ID practice.
Thrust-to-weight comes out at **1.83**, so it is not a good airframe to develop
an aggressive controller on — see the print guide for the brushless variant that
reaches 3.22.

## `import/` — for your sim

- `meshes/airframe.stl` and `.obj` — merged visual mesh, **metres**, origin at
  the airframe centre of mass, 32,832 tris
- `properties.json` — mass, CoM offset, inertia tensor
- `delivery_quad.urdf`, `delivery_quad.xml` — ready-made link/joint topology if
  useful; ignore if your sim already has its own
- `sim_export.py` — regenerates all of the above after a CAD change

| | |
|---|---|
| airframe / payload / AUW | 4.070 / 1.500 / 5.570 kg |
| inertia diag | 0.0772, 0.0772, 0.1277 kg·m² |
| CoM offset from hub centre | +8.73 mm in z |

Inertia is computed from the mass budget — motors as point masses, arms as rods,
battery, gear, cardan — not estimated. Off-diagonals are zero by symmetry for
the airframe alone.

**Model the cardan as two joints, not a fixed payload inertia.** Roll about x,
pitch about y, ±25°, light damping. With the payload rigidly attached you lose
the swing mode that will fight your controller, and you lose the configuration
dependence: at 30° of swing `I_xx` moves 6% but `I_xz` goes from exactly zero to
0.017 kg·m² — the mass matrix stops being diagonal at all.

Use collision primitives (hub box, four arm cylinders, payload box), never the
visual mesh. Mesh collision is roughly 100× slower.

## Before flying either of these

Measure mass and inertia on the finished airframe rather than trusting the CAD
figures. Expect printed parts 5–15% above the model — it assumes an 0.85 fill
factor and slicers vary. Bifilar pendulum for inertia:
`I = (m·g·d²·T²)/(16·π²·L)`. Load cell plus HX711 for thrust and drag torque.
Re-run after any airframe change, including reprinting one arm.

The delivery vehicle is 5.3 kg with 15" carbon props. Do not use it to develop a
controller that has not already been validated in sim — train, freeze, then fly
with a manual override to a known-good PID. Over 250 g needs FAA registration,
delivery is a Part 107 commercial operation, and Berkeley will have its own UAS
policy and insurance requirement.
